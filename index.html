Lesson 1.5.4 Overview

Question Structure: Effective Customer Inquiry

Move from A2 yes/no questions to B1 customer discovery. Ask purposeful questions, listen to the answers, and confirm what happened before suggesting a solution.

FORM

Build accurate yes/no, WH- and polite questions.

INVESTIGATE

Choose questions that reveal useful information.

CONFIRM

Summarize a customer's issue accurately.

Step 1 of 5 · Recommended time: 8 mins

Encounter · The Agent Who Didn't Ask Enough

Read the full interaction. Notice how assumptions and repeated solutions make the customer increasingly frustrated.

Customer Support Chat · Account Access

Customer: Hi. I can't access my account, and I need to submit an application today.

Agent: Reset your password. That usually fixes it.

Customer: I've already reset it twice. It still doesn't work.

Agent: Then restart your device and try again.

Customer: I restarted it this morning. I keep getting an error, and I'm running out of time.

Agent: You probably entered the wrong password. Please reset it again.

Customer: That's not what I'm saying! My password works on my phone, but I can't submit the application on my laptop.

Agent: If your phone works, you should just use that instead.

Customer: The application form doesn't work properly on my phone. I've explained that I need to finish it on my laptop.

Agent: You can try again later. There's nothing else I can suggest.

Customer: I've contacted you for help, and you haven't even asked what error I'm seeing. I've wasted so much time. I want to speak to your supervisor.

Spot the Breakdown

Discuss as a class: What did the agent assume? Which details were ignored? At what point did the customer become frustrated?

One Question That Could Have Helped

Individually, write one relevant question the agent could have asked to better understand the customer’s problem. You will learn how to build an effective question sequence in Clarify.

Step 2 of 5 · Recommended time: 15 mins

Clarify · Build Better Questions

Learn the grammar and use the clickable funnel to connect each question to a clear communicative purpose.

1 · Question Structures

Select a grammar pattern to reveal the structure, examples, and a common mistake.

2 · The Interactive Question Funnel

Click each level. Study its purpose, grammar, examples, and agent tip. A real conversation can move back to earlier levels whenever new information appears.

3 · Choose the Next Question

Five customer situations. Select the most useful next question at each stage of the investigation.

Step 3 of 5 · Recommended time: 12 mins

Remember · Question Builder Challenge

Focus on grammar accuracy, question purpose and responsive follow-up. Each answer has an explanation.

Part A · Fix the Question (6 items)

Choose the grammatically correct version of each customer-service question.

Part B · Ask the Most Useful Question (5 items)

Choose the question that helps the agent gather relevant information.

Part C · Listen and Follow Up (4 items)

Read the customer’s answer. Select the best next question.

Step 4 of 5 · Recommended time: 10 mins

Internalize · Build Your Own Question Funnel

Work with a partner. Analyze the case together and write the questions you would ask at each stage. Then practice the conversation aloud. Your questions should respond to what the customer says.

Case File · A Missing Refund

Customer's opening statement: “I returned an item last week, but I still haven't received my money.”

Your task: Find out what was returned, when and how it was returned, what confirmation the customer received, and what the payment status shows. Do not assume the refund has failed.

Partner Speaking Check

Suggested timing: 2 minutes analyze, 4 minutes write, 3 minutes practice, 1 minute feedback.

Step 5 of 5 · Recommended time: 15 mins

Fluent Use · Customer Detective

Discover the customer’s hidden information through conversation, then confirm the problem.

Customer Detective · Uncover the Problem

Work in pairs. One learner sees the agent brief; the other sees the customer brief. Do not show your partner your brief. Ask purposeful questions until you can accurately summarize the problem.

Only the student playing that role should click Reveal. Hide the brief before passing the screen to your partner.

Agent: Ask open, targeted, and follow-up questions. Customer: Share information when asked; do not reveal everything immediately.

Agent Case Notes

Record the issue, the key facts you discovered, and your confirmation statement.

Success criteria: mostly accurate questions, varied question types, responsive follow-ups, and a clear confirmation of the issue before recommending action.
